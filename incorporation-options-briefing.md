# Incorporation Options Briefing

## Summary

A four-page briefing from [[celine-dubois]] at the Draymoor Volunteer Centre, sent on 22 September 2026 after Hamza asked for help. Written for small community groups thinking about changing their legal form.

## Key Details

- An unincorporated association is not a legal person: contracts and leases are signed by individuals on its behalf, who may be personally liable if the club cannot pay.
- An incorporated body can own property, sign contracts and employ in its own name; members' and board members' liability is limited.
- Costs: registration fees, a new governing document, a new bank account, and more formal annual reporting.
- Assets, leases and contracts have to be transferred to the new body, which may need the landlord's and funders' consent.
- Realistic timescale for a volunteer-run group: six to twelve months.

## Why It Matters

The timescale is the problem. The Lanternfield deadline for lease evidence is 15 December 2026, so incorporating before the lease is signed is unlikely. The briefing points to a second route: sign as an association now and transfer later with the council's consent ([[should-the-club-incorporate-before-the-lease]]).

## Caveats

General guidance, not legal advice. The Volunteer Centre recommends a solicitor before any transfer of a lease.
