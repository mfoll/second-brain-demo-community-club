# Minuted Dissent

A board member who disagrees with a decision can ask for their dissent to be recorded in the minutes, in words they agree. The decision still stands, and the member still shares responsibility for carrying it out, but the record shows they argued against it and why.

## How we use it

Clause 8.6 of the [[fernhollow-constitution-2025]] gives every board member the right. The secretary drafts the wording, reads it back in the meeting, and sends the draft minutes to the member first ([[how-to-run-a-board-meeting]]).

The one time it has been used so far: [[ravinder-sahota]] on the 2026 fee rise ([[board-meeting-2026-03-10]]). His dissent named his reason (fairness to lower-income families) and a request (revisit a sliding scale after one season), which made it useful later rather than just a protest.

## Why it matters

- It lets someone lose a vote without losing face, which in a volunteer club is the difference between staying and leaving.
- It keeps the minority argument available for the next time the question comes up.
- It is not a veto, and not a reason to reopen a decision at every meeting.

Good dissent states the reason and what would change the member's mind; it does not criticise colleagues. Background on minutes in general: [[wikipedia-minutes]].
