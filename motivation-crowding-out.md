# Motivation Crowding Out

The idea that putting a price or a penalty on something people did out of goodwill can reduce how much of it they do, because the payment changes what the act means to them.

## Why I Noted It

During the [[family-volunteer-pledge]], coaches told us that some parents who paid the £30 opt-out stopped helping informally, as they had before: carrying the goals, staying to tidy up, filling in when a linesperson did not turn up. The opt-out seemed to settle the account. A few said as much: "I've paid my thirty quid."

When I described this to [[celine-dubois]] she said it was a well-known pattern, and I read the Wikipedia article ([[wikipedia-motivation-crowding-theory]]). The general idea fitted what we had seen, though our evidence is a handful of comments, not a study.

## How It Shapes What We Do

- No charges linked to volunteering.
- Thank people for help in kind, not with money or discounts on fees.
- Keep the pledge as a hope, not a contract.

## Caveat

We cannot separate this effect from simple resentment at being made to pay, or from families who were never going to help. I hold it as a likely explanation, not a proven one. See [[opt-out-fees-backfire-in-volunteer-clubs]] for the stance I took from it.
