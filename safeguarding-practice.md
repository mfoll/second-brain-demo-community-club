## How the Club's Practice Has Changed

When I became welfare officer in 2011 safeguarding at Fernhollow was one person, a folder and goodwill. The 2022 policy put the structure on paper. The 2026 review is the first time the club has looked hard at whether the paper matches what happens on a wet Saturday at Millrace Lane, and the honest answer was "mostly, apart from the building, the dates and the phones".

Three lessons I keep coming back to:

- **Routine beats vigilance.** Lapsed training was a tracking problem, not an attitude problem ([[safeguarding-should-be-boring-and-routine]]).
- **Buildings shape behaviour.** The changing-room overlap came from fixture times and walls, not from anyone's choices ([[changing-room-observation-2026-05-16]]).
- **The route must never depend on one person** ([[welfare-officer-needs-a-deputy]]).
