# Repairing Obligations

Who must repair what under a lease. In a "full repairing" lease the tenant repairs everything, including structure and roof; in an internal repairing lease the landlord keeps the outside and structure.

## Why it matters for a volunteer club

A long lease with full repairs is cheap in rent and expensive in risk. The club becomes responsible for things it cannot see coming: a roof, a drain, a boundary wall, a tree. Over 30 years, something big will fail.

## Where we landed

- Club: full repair of the pavilion and any new building, routine pitch maintenance (mowing, marking, renovation), fences and the car park surface.
- Council: main surface water drains under the pitches and boundary trees.
- The trust's grant requires us to keep the new building in good repair for 25 years, which we would have had to do anyway.

Gareth's [[pitch-condition-log-2025-26]] and Marek's costing were what moved the council on drainage. See [[millrace-lane-lease]].

## Practical consequence

We need a sinking fund for the buildings and a maintenance plan, not just goodwill ([[new-building-maintenance-plan-outline]], [[who-maintains-the-new-changing-rooms]]).
