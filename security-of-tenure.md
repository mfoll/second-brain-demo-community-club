# Security of Tenure

How long a club can be sure of staying on its site, as a funder sees it. For capital grants it is usually measured as the years left on a lease, or ownership, at the date of the grant offer.

## Working meaning

> [!info]
> A funder paying for a building wants to know the building will be used for its purpose long enough to justify the money. The Lanternfield Trust's rule is at least 25 years from the date of the offer, evidenced by a signed lease or a binding commitment from the landlord.

## Why it bites clubs like ours

- Most grassroots clubs lease from a council or a school, often on short or rolling terms. A lease that felt long when it was signed shrinks every year: our 25-year lease of 2006 has less than five years left.
- Extending a lease is a decision for the landlord's own process, on its own timetable. A council cabinet can defer for reasons that have nothing to do with the club ([[council-cabinet-deferral-2026-09-17]]).
- Funders usually also take a charge or restriction on the lease, which the landlord must consent to.

## How we are dealing with it

- A 30-year lease from 1 April 2027, so that even a grant offered in 2027 or 2028 leaves well over 25 years ([[millrace-lane-lease]]).
- Telling the funder early and honestly about the gap ([[lanternfield-tenure-deadline-email-2026-10-09]]).

## What I would tell the next chair

Look at the lease end date every year, and start any extension at least two years before you need it. We started in November 2025 for a grant in 2026 and it was not enough.
