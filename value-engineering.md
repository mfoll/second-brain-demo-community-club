# Value Engineering

Reducing the cost of a building by changing how it is designed or built, while keeping what it is for. The test is whether the saving removes something the building needs to do.

## How we used it

In June 2026 we looked for savings in the £248,000 quote and the alternatives:

- Rejected: modular build (£19,000 cheaper but no officials' room, asbestos excluded, 20 to 25-year life).
- Rejected: dropping the officials' room (about £14,000). It is a safeguarding and refereeing requirement, not a luxury.
- Rejected: one junior room instead of two. That was the whole point of the May revision.
- Accepted: standard sanitaryware and benches instead of the vandal-resistant range Neil first specified (about £3,500 saved, already in the £248,000).
- Accepted: render instead of facing brick on the side facing the pitches (about £2,000).

## Rule of thumb I took from it

Cut finishes, not functions. If a saving would make the grant case weaker or bring back a problem the building is meant to solve, it is not a saving. See [[separate-junior-changing-is-worth-the-extra-cost]].
