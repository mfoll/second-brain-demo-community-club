# No One Should Chair for More Than Six Years

## Stance

> [!info]
> A volunteer club should change its chair at least every six years, even when the chair is doing well and nobody is asking them to go. Especially then.

## Scope

Applies to the chair of a club like ours. Less so to specialist roles such as the treasurer or welfare officer, where a longer term may be the lesser evil if nobody is trained to take over, though the same risk applies.

## Why I Think This

- By year five I was the person everyone rang about everything. That felt like being useful; it was really the club becoming fragile.
- A long-serving chair shapes the board in their own image. People who would do it differently do not stand.
- Colin chaired for eleven years and handed over in three weeks. I chaired for eight and have given myself eighteen months. Neither is a model ([[coffee-with-colin-2026-01-16]]).
- The members chose six years when asked ([[constitution-review-consultation-2025]]).

## What Would Change My Mind

If, after two changes of chair under the limit, the club could not find anyone willing to stand and the board had to co-opt someone unwilling. Then the limit would be doing harm.

## Implications

- I step down in June 2027 and leave the board ([[should-hazel-stay-on-the-board-after-2027]]).
- Every chair should start a succession plan by their fourth year.
- See [[officer-term-limits]].
