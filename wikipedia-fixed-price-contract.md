# Fixed-price contract

English Wikipedia article, last edited 4 May 2026. Licence CC BY-SA 4.0.

## Summary

The article defines a fixed-price contract as one where the agreed payment is not adjusted afterwards for the resources, costs or time the contractor actually uses. It contrasts this with cost-plus contracts, which pay costs plus a margin, and with time-and-materials and labour-hour contracts.

## Why It Matters

Claire read it before the quote review on 30 June 2026 ([[changing-rooms-tender-review-2026-06-30]]). For a club with almost no reserves left after the match funding, a fixed price is the only kind of contract we can sign: any overrun would fall on members. It is also why the date on the Hutchins & Kerr quote matters so much ([[will-the-hutchins-kerr-quote-hold-until-the-grant-decision]]).

## Caveats

"Fixed" in a building quote still has exclusions and provisional sums; read the quote, not the label.
