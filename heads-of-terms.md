# Heads of Terms

A short summary of the main points of a deal, such as a lease, agreed before the lawyers draft the full document. Usually marked "subject to contract" and not legally binding.

## What ours covered

Length, start date, what land and buildings are included, rent and reviews, repairs, permitted use, community use, a funder's restriction, break clause and assignment. Eight points on two pages ([[millrace-lane-lease]]).

## What I learned

- Heads of terms are where the real negotiation happens. Once the solicitors start, changing a point of principle is slow and expensive.
- They are not enough for a funder. The Lanternfield Trust would not accept them as evidence of tenure on their own ([[security-of-tenure]]).
- For a council landlord, agreed heads of terms are only an officer's agreement. The decision still sits with cabinet.
- Write down what was agreed in each meeting and send it back to the other side the same week. Graham and I avoided two misunderstandings that way ([[lease-negotiation-notes]]).
