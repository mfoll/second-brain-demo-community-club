# Opt-Out Fees Backfire in Volunteer Clubs

## Stance

Charging families who do not volunteer costs a community club more in goodwill than it raises in money or volunteer hours. Ask specifically and personally instead, and never put a price on help.

## Scope

Community clubs run by volunteers with members who are mostly families. It may be different for a club with paid staff, where a fee funds someone to do the work.

## Why I Think This

- Our own pledge: 31 opt-outs, £930, two families lost, no improvement in rota fill rate ([[pledge-review-2026-01-09]]).
- Some payers stopped the informal help they used to give ([[motivation-crowding-out]]).
- The money came disproportionately from families working weekends, which [[ravinder-sahota]] predicted.
- [[celine-dubois]] at the Volunteer Centre had seen the same in other groups.
- I voted for the pledge in July 2025. Being wrong in public is a good teacher.

## What Would Change My Mind

A club like ours that used an opt-out fee to pay for a match-day coordinator and saw both volunteering and membership hold up. Or evidence that our role-based approach is failing worse than the pledge did.

## Implications

- I would argue against any return of a volunteer opt-out, including in the next chair's handover notes.
- Use the £930 lesson whenever someone proposes fining no-shows on the rota.
