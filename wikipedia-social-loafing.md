# Social loafing

## Summary

The English Wikipedia article on social loafing, a social psychology term for people putting in less effort towards a goal when working in a group than when working alone. The lead offers it as one main reason groups sometimes achieve less than their members would separately. Last edited 28 April 2026 at the time of the record.

## Why It Matters

[[nisha-raghavan]] cited the idea in the background to her options paper ([[role-based-recruitment-proposal-2026-01]]) to explain why "can anyone help on Saturday?" to a 140-person group chat got so little ([[volunteer-rota-2024-25-group-chat]]). Everyone assumed someone else would answer. A named ask to one person for one role removes that. It is part of the case for [[role-based-volunteering]].

## Caveats

The article is about effort in group tasks, not about answering requests for help; applying it to group chats is our stretch.
