# Safeguarding Should Be Boring and Routine

## Stance

The best safeguarding in a club is dull: dates in a system, two adults at the door, the same reporting route every time, and nobody needing to be brave or clever. Drama, whether panic or complacency, is the warning sign.

## Scope

Day-to-day safeguarding practice at Fernhollow. It does not mean concerns are handled casually; when something is raised, the welfare officer acts promptly and seriously. It means the background routines run whether or not anyone is worried.

## Why I Think This

- Nursing taught me that the safest wards were the ones with good routines that nobody had to think about: checks done, handovers written, escalation paths known.
- When the review started, 13 of 35 volunteers had lapsed training simply because nobody tracked dates ([[how-often-should-volunteers-refresh-safeguarding-training]]). Not neglect, just no routine.
- The changing-room overlap ([[changing-room-observation-2026-05-16]]) came from fixture times, not from anyone's choices. A routine fixes that; a lecture would not.

## What Would Change My Mind

If routines started to feel like box-ticking that stopped people noticing what is in front of them. Aoife watches for that, and the workshop scenarios are meant to keep judgement alive.

## Implications

- Prefer system reminders and rotas to appeals and warnings.
- Keep the policy short enough to be read.
- Report safeguarding to the board as counts and routines, never stories.
