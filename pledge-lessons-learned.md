# Pledge Lessons Learned

My own notes after the board partly reversed the [[family-volunteer-pledge]] on 10 February 2026. Written for the next chair, who will be tempted by the same idea on a bad Saturday.

## What We Hoped

That a clear expectation (three sessions per family per season) with a small cost for opting out would spread the load across about 150 families instead of 20.

## What Happened

44% of families did a session; 31 paid £30 to opt out; two families left; the rota fill rate did not move; Nisha spent four hours a week chasing ([[pledge-review-2026-01-09]]).

## What I Think Went Wrong

1. **We asked for sessions, not roles.** "Three sessions" meant nothing concrete. Families did not know what they were signing up to.
2. **We priced help.** The opt-out turned volunteering into a transaction for some families ([[motivation-crowding-out]]).
3. **One rule for different families.** Weekend shift workers, single parents and athletics families were all treated the same, and we had to bolt on exemptions.
4. **Enforcement fell on a volunteer.** Nisha became a debt collector, which is no way to treat a volunteer coordinator.
5. **I wanted a system to fix what needed conversations.** The gaps were real, but the answer was personal asks with clear roles.

## What We Kept

The pledge as a voluntary hope; the data on who helps; and the £930, which now helps families with fees through the [[hardship-bursary-fund]].
