# Volunteer Retention

Why volunteers stay at Fernhollow, why they leave, and what we have tried. A retrieval surface for the reading, the data and the lessons; the work itself lives under [[lead-the-volunteers]].

## What We Know

- Most people who stop do so quietly, at the end of a season, when their own child moves up or leaves. The [[volunteer-exit-conversations-2025]] found that only one of six left over a specific grievance.
- Load is uneven: in 2025 about seven people did more than half the volunteer hours ([[volunteer-hours-estimate-2025]]).
- Compulsion did not help. The [[family-volunteer-pledge]] raised 31 opt-out payments and lost two families, and [[pledge-lessons-learned]] sets out why.
- Small, defined asks do better than open-ended ones. See [[role-based-volunteering]] and [[volunteer-role-descriptions]].

## How the Thinking Has Moved

In 2025 we treated retention as a numbers problem: not enough people, so ask everyone. The rota overhaul made the gaps visible and the pledge tried to close them by rule. By early 2026 the picture had changed. The gaps were real, but the people leaving were mostly leaving because their child moved on or because their role had quietly grown, not because the club had failed to ask. The fixes since then are about shape rather than volume: smaller, written roles; a second person for every critical job; one-off slots as a way in; and thanks that are personal rather than staged.

The open question for 2027 is whether this survives the change of chair. Much of it depends on [[nisha-raghavan]] and on habits I have built, so the handover notes for the [[chair-succession-plan]] need to carry it.
