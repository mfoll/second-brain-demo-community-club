# Motivation crowding theory

## Summary

The English Wikipedia article on motivation crowding theory, an idea from psychology and microeconomics. The lead explains that outside incentives such as money for doing a task can sometimes weaken people's own reasons for doing it, so that total effort falls rather than rises, against what a simple economic model would predict. Last edited 7 September 2026 at the time of the record.

## Why It Matters

[[celine-dubois]] mentioned the idea when I told her about our pledge opt-out in January 2026, and I read the article before the February board. It gave a name to what coaches had been telling us: some families who paid the £30 stopped the informal help they used to give ([[pledge-review-2026-01-09]]). It shaped my write-up in [[motivation-crowding-out]] and the decision not to tie fees or discounts to volunteering.

## Caveats

The article covers rewards and incentives in general, not opt-out fees in clubs. Our own evidence is a handful of comments, so the fit is a plausible reading, not proof.
