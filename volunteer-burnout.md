# Volunteer Burnout

The wearing down of the few people who carry most of the club's work, until they stop, often suddenly and often without saying why.

## What It Means Here

In nursing I saw burnout as exhaustion, a growing detachment from the people you look after, and a sense that nothing you do is enough. In a community club it looks quieter. The same six or seven people do more than half the hours ([[volunteer-hours-estimate-2025]]). They say yes because they always have, nobody else is asked because they will do it, and the load grows until one season they do not come back. When they go, two or three jobs go with them, and nobody knew what those jobs involved.

## Why It Happens at Fernhollow

- **Open-ended asks.** "Can anyone help?" goes to the people who always help.
- **Invisible work.** Kit, first-aid restocking, rota swaps and line marking happen out of sight, so the load is never seen.
- **Roles that grow.** Tea hut lead became tea hut, tournament catering and the thank-you evening for [[helen-marsh]].
- **No handover.** Most roles had no description until 2026, so leaving felt like letting people down.
- **The chair's habit.** I have filled gaps myself for years, which hides them from the board.

## Signs I Watch For

- Someone who used to reply within the hour taking days.
- Short replies, or jokes about "when I finally escape".
- Doing the same role across two age groups as children move up.
- Missing their own child's or grandchild's match because of a club job.
- Refusing help, which is often the step before stopping.

## What the Club Changed

- [[volunteer-role-descriptions]] put limits on each role, with hours and a "what if you cannot make it" line.
- One-off slots put two helpers on the tea hut so Helen is not alone.
- [[nisha-raghavan]]'s rota shows the load: anyone on more than three slots a week gets a check-in.
- A second person for every critical role, starting with the rota admin ([[who-takes-over-the-clubfold-rota-admin]]) and the deputy welfare officer.
- A personal thank-you rather than a public one when people prefer it ([[how-do-we-recognise-volunteers-without-money]]).
- I stopped quietly doing jobs myself and started reporting gaps to the board.

## Limits

This is my working idea from experience, a bit of reading ([[wikipedia-occupational-burnout]]) and the exit conversations ([[volunteer-exit-conversations-2025]]). It is not a diagnosis, and I do not ask volunteers about their health. If someone seems genuinely unwell, the right step is a kind word and less load, not a questionnaire.
