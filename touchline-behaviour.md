# Touchline Behaviour

How adults on the side of the pitch behave during junior matches: parents, carers, coaches and anyone else watching. It covers what is shouted, at whom, and where people stand.

## In our club

Most of our touchlines are good-natured. The trouble, when it comes, is usually a handful of adults shouting instructions that contradict the coach, criticising a young referee, or standing on the pitch line so the assistant cannot see. On a Saturday at Millrace Lane, with three mini pitches close together, one loud touchline is heard by every game.

## What we ask

- Spectators stand on the far side from the coaches, behind the rope where there is one.
- Praise effort, leave instructions to the coach.
- Nobody speaks to a referee about a decision during or after a junior match. Concerns go to the team manager, who passes them to Martin as fixtures secretary.
- Coaches deal with their own touchline first; if that does not work, the match can be paused.

## Why it matters

Young referees stop refereeing when they are shouted at, and we already struggle to cover junior fixtures. Children also say, when asked, that they would rather the adults were quieter. The parents' code of conduct in the safeguarding policy says the same thing in more formal words.
