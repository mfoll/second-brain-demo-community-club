# Match Funding Appeal

Raise £23,000 from members, sponsors and events as the third part of the £63,000 match funding for the new changing rooms.

## Outcome

£23,000 raised towards the £63,000 match funding (£14,600 by 20 October 2026). The appeal closes when £23,000 is banked or pledged in writing.

## Current State

- £14,600 banked by Tuesday 20 October 2026, leaving £8,400 ([[match-funding-appeal-tracker]]).
- Where it came from: £5,000 from the summer tournament ([[summer-tournament-2026-debrief]]), £1,500 from Coyle Motors, £4,350 in member donations through the online giving page, £2,150 from the [[name-a-peg-scheme]], and £1,600 from the quiz night on 3 October ([[appeal-quiz-night-2026-10-03]]).
- The other two parts are secure: £25,000 from reserves (board, 12 May 2026) and £15,000 from the Draymoor Community Chest ([[community-chest-award-2026-08-20]]). See [[match-funding]] for how the whole £63,000 fits together.
- The trust application counts the appeal as pledged, so the gap must close before any grant offer, which means by about February 2027.

## Scope and Boundaries

The appeal only: events, donations, sponsorship asks and the tracker. The reserves commitment is Claire's and the board's, under [[manage-club-money]]. Fee income is not appeal money, although the 2026 fee rise ([[board-meeting-2026-03-10]]) was partly argued on the basis that the appeal could not carry everything. We do not ask juniors to fundraise door to door, and we do not publish donors' names without their say-so.

## Working Context

We launched at the end-of-season presentation evening in June with a leaflet ([[appeal-leaflet-2026-06]]) and a thermometer on the pavilion wall that Yusuf painted. The tournament was always going to be the big one. Sam and the board agreed in advance that £5,000 of the net would go to the appeal and the rest to general funds; the tournament netted £8,900, so that held.

Donations came in a burst after the leaflet and slowed by August. The name-a-peg idea came from Helen: £25 puts a family or team name on a coat peg plate in the new junior rooms. It is popular with grandparents and teams, less so with adult players.

Coyle Motors already sponsor the kit at £1,500 a year. Brian Coyle gave a separate £1,500 to the appeal in July, on condition it is not mistaken for the kit money; Claire banks them separately. Asking him again is not on the cards.

What is left, £8,400, is the hard part. My guess is that another £3,000 to £4,000 can come from events (Christmas raffle, a sponsored walk in March) and the rest has to come from two or three local businesses or a second small grant. I have not decided whether to ask the members for a second round; the fee rise is still fresh ([[ravinder-sahota]] has said plainly he would be against it).

There is also the question of whether £23,000 is enough. The quote is fixed, but if the build slips into 2028 prices, the gap falls on us ([[should-the-appeal-target-include-a-contingency]]).
