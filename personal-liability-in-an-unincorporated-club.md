# Personal Liability in an Unincorporated Club

An unincorporated members' club is not a legal person. When it signs a lease, a contract or a loan, real people sign on its behalf, and if the club cannot meet the obligation, those people may be asked to. For most of a club's life this risk is small: fees cover costs and insurance covers accidents. It grows with long commitments.

## Why it matters now

Fernhollow is about to take on two of the biggest commitments in its history: a 30-year lease of Millrace Lane ([[pitch-lease-extension]]) and a £248,000 building contract ([[new-changing-rooms]]). [[marek-zielinski]] asked at the September 2026 board who would be on the hook if a contractor failed halfway or the club folded in year 20. The honest answer was "probably whoever signed" ([[board-meeting-2026-09-08]]).

## What reduces it

- Incorporating, so the club itself holds the lease and contract ([[incorporation-options-briefing]]).
- Fixed-price contracts with retentions, and grant money held until stages are certified.
- Insurance for board members, which covers some claims but not debts.
- Not signing alone: the constitution requires two signatories.

General background in [[wikipedia-unincorporated-association]]. This is my understanding as a lay chair, not legal advice; the review will need a solicitor.
